The app now works for several Navidrome users: each playlist goes to the users you choose, as their own playlist. History and mixes per user come next.

  • Navidrome accounts. Settings → Navidrome accounts lists the Navidrome users this app works for. Add one with the address of your Navidrome, a user name and its password, test the connection, edit it, make it the default, or remove it.
  • Your install becomes the first account. It keeps working exactly as before, with its .m3u8 playlists, history and mixes. Add its address and password when you want it to be ready for per-user playlists.
  • A picker per browser. With two accounts or more, a picker at the top of the page chooses the account this browser works on. Another browser, or the phone, keeps its own choice.
  • Playlists for each user. A playlist goes to the accounts ticked under In Navidrome in its drawer. For each one, it is published as that user's own private playlist through Navidrome's API, kept up to date in place, and tracks Navidrome has not scanned yet are added as soon as it has.
  • Your playlists stay as they are until you choose. Your install's account keeps writing .m3u8 files, which Navidrome gives to its first admin. Publish as this user… in Settings → Navidrome accounts moves them to the API: the app publishes them, then removes its .m3u8 files and the copies Navidrome had imported from them. If Navidrome refuses one, nothing changes.
  • Per account in the dashboard. Playlists added go to the account of this browser, and the dashboard shows that account's playlists. Adding a playlist another account already has shares it. Delete takes a shared playlist away from this account only.
  • Deleting removes it from Navidrome. A deleted playlist now also leaves Navidrome: the playlist this app made there, or its .m3u8 file. Navidrome keeps a playlist it imported even once the file is gone, so that copy is removed too when the account has its password; otherwise the app says to delete it in Navidrome.
  • Encrypted credentials. Passwords are kept encrypted, and so are the secrets already saved in secrets.json (Deezer ARLs, tokens). None of them is ever shown again or written in a log.

After the update

  • For stronger protection, set D2N_ENCRYPTION_KEY to a long random string. Without it, the key is kept in /data/encryption.key, so a copy of the whole data folder carries it along.
  • Going back to an older version means entering your Deezer ARL and tokens again: older versions cannot read the encrypted values.
  • Navidrome does not accept API keys yet: sign accounts in with their password.
  • Publishing through the API finds each track by its file path in Navidrome's database, which must be mounted at /import/navidrome, as for the history import. Without it, tracks are searched by their tags, which misses some.
  • The app replaces its playlists in Navidrome at each update: a change made to them in Navidrome is lost at the next one.
  • Your saved playlists are first copied to /data/backups/pre-1.16.0/.
